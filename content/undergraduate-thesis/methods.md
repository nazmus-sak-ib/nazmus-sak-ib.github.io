## Asking travelers

Because the pandemic prevented station-based interviews, the team used an online questionnaire circulated through social media. **1,088 people responded**. Buses accounted for about **93%** of reported main-mode choices, leading the analysis to focus on air-conditioned and non-air-conditioned services.

## Preparing the comparison

We organized respondents' background and trip information, examined distributions and outliers, and compared service choices across travel frequency, income, companions, trip purpose, and time of day. The thesis reports an analysis of **1,011 bus respondents** after narrowing the sample.

## Modeling choice

We developed and compared a series of **logit choice models**: statistical models that connect trip characteristics and passenger circumstances with the probability of choosing a service. Successive versions tested travel time, fare, income, and group differences, retaining useful terms in a final specification.

## Important limits

Online recruitment was not representative of everyone using the route. Some groups, including students and people with internet access, were prominent. The positive fare coefficient for air-conditioned buses was counterintuitive and could reflect unmeasured quality or sample selection. Comfort and safety ratings were collected but were not directly included in the final model.
