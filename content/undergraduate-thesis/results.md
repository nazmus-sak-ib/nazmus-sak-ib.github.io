## The short version

- Respondents using air-conditioned services were more likely to prioritize comfort: **86.8%**, compared with **57.85%** among non-air-conditioned users in the reported figure.
- Longer travel time reduced the modeled appeal of both services, with a larger negative coefficient for non-air-conditioned buses.
- Higher income was associated with lower modeled preference for the non-air-conditioned service.

## Reading the results carefully

The figures describe the surveyed groups, not all route users. The model also produced an unexpected positive association between air-conditioned fares and choice. We discussed service quality and sampling as possible explanations; the analysis did not establish that raising fares would increase demand.

The thesis recommended a more balanced station-based sample and further work incorporating comfort and safety into the model.
