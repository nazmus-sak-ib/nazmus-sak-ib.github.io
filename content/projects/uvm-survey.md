## The question

What makes an electric vehicle a less appealing choice for a rural household? My thesis examines everyday concerns such as charging, range, and vehicle suitability alongside environmental values, scientific trust, autonomy, and social norms, using a survey of residents in Vermont and Maine.

## My contribution

As the project's sole graduate student, I have taken primary responsibility for literature synthesis and survey instrument development under my PI's supervision. I brought together transportation, psychology, and political-studies literature to develop questions about what people need from a vehicle and how their beliefs relate to their willingness to adopt an EV.

## Study and sample

The **UNH Survey Center** administered the online survey in Vermont and Maine in summer 2026. We received **1,684 responses**, with **56% rural and 44% urban** respondents before analytical screening.

## Analysis underway

I am using structural equation modeling to examine how practical concerns and social-psychological factors relate to adoption intention. The work includes checking the survey measures, comparing rural and urban groups, and using dominance analysis to assess the relative importance of the factors. I also plan to link the responses with geographic data.

## Early observations

Rural respondents were less open to a battery-electric vehicle as their next vehicle, and their assessments of vehicle suitability also differed from those of urban respondents. These initial survey comparisons were presented at the VTrans symposium; the structural-model analysis is still underway.
