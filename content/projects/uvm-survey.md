## The question

Is the rural EV gap mainly about whether the vehicle fits daily life, or do values, trust, and social expectations help explain it too? My thesis brings these questions together instead of treating charging access and psychology as separate stories.

## My contribution

As the project's sole graduate student, I have taken primary responsibility for literature synthesis and survey instrument development under my PI's supervision. I brought together transportation, psychology, and political-studies literature to connect practical perceptions with values, beliefs, and norms.

## Study and sample

The online survey was administered by the **UNH Survey Center** in Vermont and Maine in summer 2026. The supplied symposium poster reports **1,684 responses**, with **56% rural and 44% urban** respondents. The current analytical sample may differ after screening.

## Analysis underway

I am working with structural equation modeling to examine relationships among practical perceptions, social-psychological constructs, and adoption intention. The analysis plan includes measurement checks and rural–urban comparisons; dominance analysis explores relative importance. Spatial integration remains an area for further development.

## Early observations

The poster describes lower openness to a battery-electric next vehicle among rural respondents and differences in perceived vehicle suitability. These are preliminary descriptive patterns, not final structural-model findings or causal effects.
