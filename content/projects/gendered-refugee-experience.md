## Why I wrote it

For my Development Studies coursework, I explored how displacement affects women and men differently—not only through immediate violence, but through the opportunities and constraints that shape everyday survival.

The essay focuses on the Rohingya context and the difficult distinction between survival sex, trafficking, and other forms of exploitation. It asks what gets lost when those experiences are treated as interchangeable.

## Abstract synopsis

Extended displacement can restrict access to work, safety, and essential resources. This essay considers how those restrictions interact with gendered roles and power to expose refugee women and girls to exploitation. Drawing on literature and published reports, it examines accounts involving promised marriage, work, security, or escape from camp restrictions, while acknowledging that the available evidence does not conclusively establish the circumstances of each case.

## Approach and limits

This is an exploratory **course essay**, completed December 12, 2022, rather than an externally published article or interview study. It uses secondary literature and news accounts. Underreporting, ambiguous reporting, and the absence of first-person interviews limit the conclusions.
